There is a feeling we get from time to time after some news. It hurts our heart. It gives us a stomachache. Before sleeping we think about it and feel sorry. It is hard to explain, but I hope many readers know this feeling. This is the feeling I want to write about.

I can give one example from the pandemic. There was news from California about a 17-year-old who had died because urgent care rejected him. After a few days we learned the truth: the boy had insurance and died in the hospital after six long hours. Still, that first story felt possible, and that is the part that hurts.

I also remember a mother crying on the news because she could not provide food for her child. She said the child fell asleep while saying, “I am hungry.”

But after some time, we forget what happened because other problems arrive. Work, school, relationships, family issues — the list goes on. We all have lives, and I do not want people to feel sorry only briefly while other injustices continue.

## What is social justice?

Social justice is the belief that everyone deserves equal rights, opportunities, and fair treatment, regardless of their background. It is about breaking down unfair barriers so that wealth, education, and healthcare are shared more fairly, and no one is marginalized because of who they are.

This paragraph is easy to say, and most people already know this definition. But sometimes we face problems that remind us social justice is not here yet. Sometimes events happen that hurt other people and become a reminder that social justice is still missing.

After those events, we often feel powerless. We are still fighting for our own food and dealing with injustice in one way or another. How can we change the whole system?

## It is not true.

Especially after my baby was born, I started to worry about the future of humanity and the world. I can see that in the last 200 years we have gained a lot. We are still dealing with many problems, but we have earned a lot too. Still, it is not enough, and we deserve better.
